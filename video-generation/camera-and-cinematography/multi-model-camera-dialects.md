## 1. Executive Overview

Different AI video diffusion models interpret camera directions through distinct tokenizers, training datasets, and conditioning architectures. What works flawlessly in **Hailuo AI** (naturalistic conversational directions) will fail or be ignored in **Wan 2.1** (which requires strict linear spatial ordering) or **Kling 4.0** (which uses structured asset-role tagging and explicit timeline brackets).

This guide is the **Universal Rosetta Stone** for AI camera syntax, translating core cinematographic moves across all major video engines.

## 3. Model-Specific Dialect Breakdown

### 3.1 Kling AI 4.0: The Timeline & Reference Dialect
Kling 4.0 parses camera instructions best when broken into sequence brackets or referenced from motion assets.
- **Syntax Formula:**
  ```
  [0-2s]: Medium close-up, eye level, static locked shot.
  [2-5s]: Camera initiates smooth lateral tracking shot left, pacing subject.
  [Audio]: Wind howling, gravel crunching under boots.
  ```
- **Motion Transfer Syntax:**
  ```
  Reference the camera path and pacing of @Video1. Replace the subject with @Image1.
  ```

### 3.2 Higgsfield (Cinema Studio & Seedance): The Rig Preset Dialect
Higgsfield recognizes standardized Hollywood mechanical rig names directly as system tokens.
- **Rule:** Never write vague terms like "cinematic camera move." Use exact hardware terms:
  ```
  Camera Dolly In, FPV Drone, Snorricam, 360 Orbit, Crane Up, Levitation, Through Object In.
  ```
- **Syntax Formula:**
  ```
  Modern 35mm Anamorphic lens, f/2.0. Camera Dolly In toward the subject over 6 seconds, locked horizon, zero distortion.
  ```

### 3.3 Hailuo AI (MiniMax): The Director-to-Crew Dialect
Hailuo AI responds poorly to bracketed code syntax (`[CAM: Pan]`), but excels when spoken to like a real camera operator on a movie set.
- **Rule:** Specify shot size first, followed by subject action, setting, and exact crew movement with speed and amplitude.
- **Syntax Formula:**
  ```
  Medium close-up of a cybernetic warrior resting inside a neon-lit rain shelter. The camera pushes in slowly with small amplitude handheld jitter, keeping his eyes centered in the frame.
  ```

### 3.4 Wan 2.1: The Linear Progressive Dialect
Wan 2.1 processes tokens sequentially along a spatial timeline. Describe what the camera does *as* the action unfolds, especially for reveals.
- **Core Formula:** `[Camera Motion] + [Subject Details] + [Action] + [Scene Reveal]`
- **Syntax Formula:**
  ```
  Close-up of a bronze key resting on a wooden table. The camera pulls back slowly and tilts upward to reveal a vast gothic library illuminated by stained glass windows.
  ```

### 3.5 CogVideoX (5B / 1.5): The Vector Cardinal Dialect
CogVideoX requires concise, directional motion keywords integrated into natural sentences.
- **Allowed Motion Keywords:** `zoom in`, `zoom out`, `pan left`, `pan right`, `tilt up`, `tilt down`.
- **Syntax Formula:**
  ```
  A wide shot of a solitary lighthouse on a stormy coast as the camera pans right and tilts down, following the breaking ocean waves against the jagged rocks.
  ```

### 3.6 HunyuanVideo (Tencent): The Spatial Spec Dialect
HunyuanVideo features native recognition of shot types and technical specs. Integrate the camera spec into the middle or closing clause.
- **Syntax Formula:**
  ```
  A cozy alpine cabin in a snowy pine valley at twilight, smoke rising gently from the stone chimney, warm yellow interior glow through windows, slow aerial approach tracking forward, smooth 24fps motion cadence.
  ```

## 5. Golden Rules for Cross-Engine Prompt Portability

1. **Decouple Framing from Movement:** Always specify the opening framing (*Wide, Medium, Close-up*) before specifying the camera travel vector (*Dolly In, Pan Right*).
2. **Never Compound Opposing Vectors:** Never instruct a model to zoom in while pulling back unless explicitly targeting the Hitchcock Dolly Zoom effect.
3. **State the Endpoint Reveal:** If the camera moves, declare what it lands on at the end of the duration so the diffusion scheduler has an endpoint boundary condition.
